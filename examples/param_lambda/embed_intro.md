# Embedding test: λ(x) with 31 CVs

Can the pipeline (M2 screening, M1, M3) find the reaction coordinate among many CVs? Does λ stay smooth along the
paths when the inputs jitter, and does a slow coordinate that does not affect the committor stay out of it?
Generated by `examples/param_lambda/embed_report.py` from `run_embedded.sh` runs. The configs are `z_emb`,
`cc_emb` and `cc_emb_np`, and the method is described in `embed.py`.

## Findings

1. **With good labels, feature selection works.**
   - M2 ranks y and x first, then the copies, and gives the 20 fast coordinates and w zero importance.
   - M3 on all 31 CVs puts 95–99% of the per-step motion of λ on y and x.
   - The slow coordinate w moves logit λ by at most 0.004 over its spread.
   - M1 L1-logistic keeps exactly y and x.
2. **Cost of the extra CVs in accuracy.**
   - cc: M3-fast (64×64, β = 10) matches the 2D run (spread 0.018 against 0.018).
   - Z: spread 0.072 against 0.055.
   - The dynamics are as good as in 2D: test SD(Z_q) 2.1 (Z) and 3.5 (cc).
3. **Without the clean y and x, λ jitters, and no setting fixes that.**
   - The step ratio rises to 1.5–2.6 (0.75–0.85 with y and x).
   - SD(Z_q) rises to 15–22, so the selection metric flags it at once.
   - Z fails (spread 0.52); cc degrades (0.08).
   - The network leans on the least noisy copy (cy1: 70%). Combining copies cannot remove noise of about 2 RMS steps, and β = 10 does not help, because the smooth signal is not in the inputs.
   - Since λ must be memoryless, noisy CVs have to be made smooth in space (switching functions, centroids), not in time.
4. **End to end, the labels are the weak point, and the slow coordinate leaks in through them.**
   - NPNEq on all 31 CVs (40000 iterations, 52 min) gives a q_np with spread 0.092 against the exact committor (0.0185 for the 2D fit).
   - Its per-path logit offsets are 15× larger (SD 0.31 against 0.02).
   - A function of w explains 25% of those offsets (1% for w shuffled between paths). NPNEq used the coordinate that labels the paths to fit path-level noise, and M2 ranks w third.
   - M3 inherits this: w moves logit λ by 0.29–0.32, about 1.5 slab half-widths, which shifts the interfaces from path to path. M1 is affected less (0.035).
   - In N-D, an NPNEq fit therefore needs path-constant CVs screened out first (within-path against between-path variance), or a check that shuffles them between paths.
   - For the tunnelling case this means a slow coordinate that matters and one that only labels paths look the same in the labels. Telling them apart needs held-out dynamics, or runs where w is coupled.
5. **M3-fast** (the smallest network within 10% of the best validation SD(Z_q); here 64×64, β = 10) is the best or tied on cc_emb and z_emb. Its jitter share outside y and x is the lowest (0.6% on cc). On cc_emb_np it is worse (0.097 against 0.070).
6. **Per-frame cost with 31 CVs** (one-frame calls, single-threaded BLAS):

   | model | µs/frame |
   |---|---|
   | 64×64 | 10.5–11.5 |
   | 128×128×128 | 17 |
   | M1 logistic | 4–5 |
   | M1 GAM (31 splines) | 17 |

   Training takes 13–115 s per M3 run. NPNEq on 31 CVs is the expensive step: 52 min on cc with the GPU shared. Z takes 0.28 s per iteration (21M frames), so 10× its 2D fit's 25000 iterations would be about 19 h on the laptop GPU: a job for the workstation.

## Setup

### What is added, and where

**Nothing in the simulations changes.** The toytis runs, the dynamics of (y, x), the states, the q_np labels
(except in `cc_emb_np`) and the exact committor are those of the 2D runs.
- The extra coordinates are added afterwards, to the frame cache of the 2D run, by `embed.py`
  (`python run.py embed z_emb`). It reads `<out>/z_br/frames.npz` and writes `<out>/z_emb/frames.npz`.
- That new cache has 31 CV columns per frame instead of 2: y and x unchanged in columns 1–2, then 29 new ones.
- Everything after that (screen, fit, evaluate) runs on the 31-column cache exactly as on the 2D one.

| group | CVs | definition | what it tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| relevant | y, x | the original positions, unchanged | the coordinates the committor depends on |
| noisy copies | cy1, cy3, cy10 · cx1, cx3, cx10 · cu3, cv3 | c = y + a·ξ(t) (or x, u = (y + x)/√2, v = (y − x)/√2), a = 0.01, 0.03, 0.1 (the digits in the name are a in units of 0.01); each copy has its own ξ, a fast OU process with relaxation time 2 frames and SD 1 | redundant, correlated inputs that jitter from frame to frame |
| fast irrelevant | f01 … f20 | OU processes with relaxation times 1, 2, 5, 10, 20 frames (cycled), SD 0.1 (about the SD of y and x) | feature selection: inputs that carry no information |
| slow | w | an OU process with relaxation time 10⁶ frames, SD 0.1 | a coordinate that is nearly constant along each path (see below) |

**Noisy copies are extra columns, not distorted y and x.**
- y and x themselves are never changed. A copy such as cy1 is a separate column: y measured with fast noise.
- The noise is in that input only. The dynamics, the states (A and B from the clean y) and the labels do not
  see it.
- How large the noise is: one MD step moves y by 0.0045 (Z) or 0.0071 (cc) RMS. So a = 0.01 is noise of about
  1.5–2 steps per frame, and a = 0.1 about 15–20 steps.
- In the `cvs` feature set the model gets y, x and all the copies, and can learn to ignore the noisy ones.
- In the `noclean` set it gets everything except y and x. Then its only access to the reaction coordinate is
  through noisy copies. This is the jitter test, the case of real CVs that fluctuate on the time step (a
  distance that vibrates, a coordination number counted with a sharp cutoff).

### The OU process

An Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process is Brownian motion in a harmonic well. Sampled at the saved frames it is an AR(1)
series:

ξ(t + 1) = ρ ξ(t) + √(1 − ρ²) · σ · η(t), with ρ = exp(−1/τ), η(t) independent standard normal.

- The series starts from the stationary distribution, ξ(0) ~ N(0, σ²), so every frame is N(0, σ²).
- Its autocorrelation decays as exp(−k/τ) over k frames. τ is the relaxation time in frames, σ the SD.
- **Fast** (τ = 1–20 frames): a new, nearly independent value every few frames, so it jitters along a path.
- **Slow** (τ = 10⁶ frames): it hardly moves within a path (paths are at most 6526 frames long on Z).
- The process is time-reversible, so a reversed segment is as valid as a forward one.

### How the values are attached to the frames

- One independent realization per coordinate and per path, generated over the path's own frames
  0, 1, …, len − 1 (`embed._ou`).
- Each cached frame looks up the value of its physical frame:
  - forward piece: frame t;
  - mirrored piece: frame t_last − t, because a mirrored piece is the start of the path read backwards.
- So a frame that appears in both pieces of a path gets the same values in both. This was checked on every
  shared frame: 469k on cc.
- The realizations are seeded (`[embed] seed`), so the cache is reproducible.

### The slow coordinate w

- **What it is:** a stand-in for a slow degree of freedom orthogonal to the reaction, like a side chain or a lid
  that rearranges much more slowly than a path lasts.
- **How it behaves:** it has a relaxation time of 10⁶ frames, so along any one path it is effectively a constant,
  drawn anew for each path from N(0, 0.1²).
- **In this first test it is not coupled:** it is not in the potential and the committor does not depend on it.
  The right λ ignores it.
- **The risk it probes:** because w is constant along a path but different between paths, it is a *path label*.
  The q_np labels have path-correlated errors (a whole path's q_np can be a bit high or low), and a model can fit
  those errors through w. The resulting λ then shifts its interfaces from path to path.
- **The tunnelling case** is different: a slow coordinate that does change the committor (a hidden barrier or
  gate). It needs new runs with a coupled potential.

**Why appending afterwards is exact.**
- The extra coordinates do not interact with (y, x), so the probability of a path factorizes into a (y, x) part
  and an extra-coordinate part. Attaching independent stationary realizations to the existing paths therefore
  samples the 31-D path ensemble exactly.
- The exact committor is still q_2D(y, x).
- Not reproduced: in a real run, w would carry over from path to path through the shooting points, which
  correlates it along the MC chain. That also needs new runs.

### Labels and fits

**Labels.**
- `z_emb` and `cc_emb` use the 2D q_np of the base run. The labels are good, so only the inputs change.
- `cc_emb_np` refits q_np with NPNEq on all 31 columns (40000 iterations, about 10× the 2D fit; `run.py
  fit-qnp-embedded`): the end-to-end test.

**Fits.**
- M2 screening on the 31 raw CVs.
- M1 on the top-2 and top-8 screened sets, on all CVs, and on all but y and x.
- M3: the lean sweep (64×64 and 128×128×128, β ∈ {0, 10}) on `cvs` and on `noclean`.
- Selection as in the main report: validation SD(Z_q), plus M3-fast, the smallest within 10%.
- The 2D comparison is the base run's own lean sweep on (y, x), with the same rule.

### How to read the numbers

- **The metrics, data, weights and splits** are those of the main report: [How to read this
  report](param_lambda_report.md#how-to-read-this-report).
  - They are computed on the test paths, using both forward and mirrored pieces and rejection weights.
  - The stopped Z_q is `optimalrcs/metrics_tis.py`.
  - The exact committor is q_2D(y, x).
  - Every candidate is calibrated on its system's q_np, so in `cc_emb_np` even the exact committor's numbers move
    (it is calibrated on the 31-D q_np).
- **Two attributions** say which CVs a model actually uses. Both come from the model's gradient ∂z/∂x_i at the
  test frames with 1e-3 < q_exact < 0.99:
  - *Jitter share*: the share of CV i in the per-step motion of the score, E[(∂z/∂x_i · Δx_i)²] over lag-1 steps,
    normalized to sum to 1. A model that uses a noisy copy gets a large share for it, because the copy moves a lot
    from frame to frame.
  - *Static share and the w effect*: E[(∂z/∂x_i · SD(x_i))²], how much z changes when x_i varies over its spread.
    w hardly moves along a path, so it never shows in the jitter share. Its static share, and the RMS change of
    logit λ over the spread of w, show whether λ uses it as a per-path offset. For scale: the isosurface slabs are
    ±0.2 logit wide.
